A Script header

Core concept

A complete written instruction needs an explanation designed for the second moment: when a patient must use it.

Mood

Quiet, calm and reassuring, like an ordinary evening at home. Slow pacing, soft music, paper and pencil sounds. A small moment of confusion, not a dramatic medical story.

Theme

Correct, complete information in the room may be difficult to use alone at home.

Buyer question

Which multi-step home instructions need explanations designed for the moment of use, beyond complete written instructions?

Visual system

  • Orange

  • Clean sans labels

  • Orange accents
  • Yellow paper

  • Progressive reveals over complex morphs

  • Light music
  • Paper & pencil SFX
  • Silent beats marked in the audio column
  • Ends on a 3s logo

01

The Second Moment

Executive lens

3 points

  • “Correct in the room and unusable at the kitchen table.”

    →The central insight of the raw content source, and a scene a buyer can picture without any clinical detail.

  • “The written version is complete”, and the same instructions in the order written vs the order done.

    →Shows F.Learning’s value as information design without blaming the client’s existing documents.

  • A clear leaflet is enough for a simple instruction; this work “earns its cost” for several steps at home.

    →Answers the executive’s cost question honestly. Saying where the service does not fit makes the rest more credible.

02

The Question That Keeps Coming Back

Project Lead lens

4 points

  • Steps 1 and 3: find where confusion happens, then choose the format by literacy, language and emotional context.

    →Gives the project lead a method rather than a list of formats.

  • AMD: clinicians spent less time re-explaining basics and more on care decisions.

    →The only result about clinician time in the raw content source, and it matches the repeated-question story. Used in its own words, with no numbers added.

  • NYU Langone Health: multilingual colonoscopy-preparation education.

    →The clearest real case of adapting one clinical source for patients who cannot rely on written English.

  • Client clinicians sign off accuracy; F.Learning reviews whether a patient can still follow it.

    →Answers the project lead’s main risk question in a single beat.

Both videos · CTA

“Book a 30-minute patient education call”: 30 minutes with a learning designer, about one difficult instruction. No deck required.

→A low-effort next step for a buyer who is already evaluating options.
